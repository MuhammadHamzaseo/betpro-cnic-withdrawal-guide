Two questions come up again and again from Pakistani BetPro users of every age: "Do I need my CNIC for BetPro withdrawals?" and "Can I withdraw to my brother's or father's account?" Both touch on something important: the connection between your BetPro ID, the name on your mobile wallet or bank account, and your identity. Getting this wrong can delay withdrawals, cause disputes with your desk, or even expose your family members to problems they never signed up for.

This guide explains, in plain language, how identity usually works with BetPro desks, why wallet names matter, when a desk might ask for proof, how to share information safely, and why withdrawing to relatives' accounts is usually a bad idea. It also covers what to do if you've already used someone else's wallet, how to change wallets properly, and how to protect your CNIC from misuse by scammers.

Do You Need a CNIC to Use BetPro?

Most BetPro desks in Pakistan don't ask for a CNIC just to open an ID. Registration typically needs a name, a username, a phone number and a first deposit. Portals like betproidpakistan.com can create an ID in about two minutes on that basis.

However, your identity is still involved indirectly. In Pakistan, mobile wallets such as Easypaisa and JazzCash, and bank accounts, are registered in a person's name and linked to their CNIC. When you deposit from your wallet and withdraw back to it, the wallet's name effectively identifies you. That's why desks care about names matching.

Why Wallet Names Matter

  • Proving ownership: if you forget your password or lose your phone, deposits from a wallet in your name help prove the ID is yours.
  • Preventing fraud: desks want to avoid deposits from stolen or third-party accounts, which can later be reversed or reported.
  • Smooth withdrawals: paying winnings to the same name that deposited reduces disputes.
  • Protecting you: if a withdrawal goes to your own account, nobody else can claim it.

When Might a Desk Ask for Proof?

Some desks may ask for extra confirmation in specific situations, for example:

  • A large withdrawal compared with your usual activity.
  • A withdrawal to an account name different from the one that deposited.
  • A dispute over who owns the ID.
  • A deposit that was reported or reversed.

Reasonable requests usually involve confirming your registered number, sharing deposit receipts, or confirming the wallet name. Be very careful about sending full CNIC images, and never send them to anyone who contacted you first or who isn't your known desk.

Can You Withdraw to a Brother's or Father's Account?

Technically, some desks may agree to send money to a relative's wallet. In practice, it's usually a bad idea:

  • Delays and refusals: many desks prefer, or require, withdrawals to the same name that deposited.
  • Disputes: if the relative disagrees about the money or the account is closed, you have little recourse.
  • Family involvement: your relative's wallet becomes linked to betting activity they may know nothing about.
  • Account reviews: unusual incoming transfers can trigger questions from the wallet provider to your relative, not to you.
  • Trust and privacy: it's hard to keep betting private when money flows through family accounts.

The cleaner approach is simple: open a wallet in your own name, deposit only from it, and withdraw only to it. That one habit removes most identity questions before they ever come up.

What If You Don't Have Your Own Wallet?

If you're an adult without your own wallet, opening one is usually straightforward with your own SIM and CNIC through the wallet provider's official app or franchise. If you can't open one, for example because you're under 18, that's a clear sign you shouldn't be betting at all. BetPro is for adults only.

If You've Already Used Someone Else's Wallet

  1. Tell your desk honestly which account you used, whose it is, and why.
  2. Ask whether they can note that the wallet belongs to a family member, or whether you should switch to your own.
  3. Open your own wallet and use it for all future deposits and withdrawals.
  4. Keep receipts for the old deposits in case ownership of the ID is ever questioned later.
  5. Make sure your relative knows about the transactions and agrees, to avoid misunderstandings or hurt feelings.

Protecting Your CNIC

Your CNIC is extremely valuable to fraudsters. With a clear photo, they can attempt to open accounts, register SIMs or take out loans in your name. Protect it:

  • Don't send CNIC photos to anyone you don't know and trust, however official they seem.
  • Never send CNIC photos to "support" accounts that contacted you first.
  • If you must share for a legitimate reason, consider covering parts that aren't needed and adding a watermark such as "For [purpose] only, [date]".
  • Don't post CNIC images in groups or public chats, even to "prove" your side in a dispute with a dealer.
  • If you think your CNIC has been misused, contact your bank and wallet provider and consider reporting it.

Common Scams Involving CNIC and Withdrawals

"Verification Fee" Scams

You're told your withdrawal needs "CNIC verification" and a fee. Genuine desks don't charge for withdrawals.

Fake Support Asking for Documents

After you post about a problem, someone claiming to be BetPro support asks for your CNIC, a selfie and an OTP. This is identity theft.

"Tax Clearance" Demands

Scammers claim FBR or government taxes must be paid before release. Government agencies don't collect tax through a dealer's wallet.

Money Mule Requests

Someone asks to "use your wallet" to receive betting money for them, sometimes offering a small payment. Your account could be flagged for fraud, and you could be held responsible.

How Deposits and Withdrawals Flow

It helps to picture how money moves when you use a BetPro desk:

  1. You send rupees from your wallet or bank account to the desk's account.
  2. The desk confirms the money arrived and credits the same amount to your BetPro ID.
  3. You bet; your balance goes up or down as markets settle.
  4. You request a withdrawal from your available balance.
  5. The desk sends rupees from its account to the wallet you name.

At steps 1 and 5, the account names are the only link between your betting ID and a real person. When the same name appears at both ends, everything is clear. When different names appear, questions arise: whose money is it, who controls the ID, and who is responsible if something goes wrong? That's why keeping one name throughout matters so much.

Lending your wallet, even to a friend, can make you part of problems you know nothing about. The safe answer to "can I use your account?" is always no.

Overseas Users and Family Wallets

Pakistanis working abroad often use Pakistani desks and route money through a relative's wallet at home. It feels convenient, but it combines several risks: mismatched names, family involvement, and the laws of the country you live in. If you're overseas, use an account in your own name wherever possible, keep your own records, and don't ask family members in Pakistan to receive or send betting money for you. The betting laws of your country of residence also apply to you, so understand them before betting at all.

Joint Accounts and Business Accounts

Avoid using joint bank accounts, business accounts or a shop's wallet for betting. Joint accounts involve another person's money and name. Business accounts mix personal betting with business transactions, which can cause problems with partners, accountants and banks. Keep betting money in a personal wallet that holds only what you've decided you can afford to lose.

Keeping Records That Protect You

Good records make identity questions easy to resolve. For every deposit and withdrawal, save:

  • The date and time.
  • The amount.
  • The transaction ID.
  • The sending and receiving account names.

Store screenshots in one folder. If your desk ever needs to confirm that an ID is yours, or if you need to recover access after losing your phone, these records are usually enough without any need for CNIC images.

Signs a "Desk" Is Really an Identity Thief

  • It asks for your CNIC before you've even deposited.
  • It wants a selfie holding your CNIC for a small withdrawal.
  • It asks for OTPs, PINs or your wallet login.
  • It contacted you first after you posted about a problem.
  • It has no website, only a WhatsApp number.
  • It charges a fee for "verification".

Any one of these is reason to stop. Several together mean you're almost certainly dealing with a scammer.

Changing Your Withdrawal Wallet

Sometimes you genuinely need to change wallets, for example because you switched networks or closed an old account. To do it smoothly:

  1. Tell your desk before requesting a withdrawal to the new wallet.
  2. Make sure the new wallet is in your own name.
  3. If possible, make a small deposit from the new wallet first, so the desk sees the same name at both ends.
  4. Keep receipts from both the old and new wallets.
  5. Expect a little extra checking on the first withdrawal to the new wallet. That protects you too.

Family Conversations

If family members' accounts are already involved in your betting, a calm, honest conversation is better than letting them find out through an unexpected SMS or a wallet review. Explain what happened, agree to stop using their accounts, and move everything to your own wallet. If betting is becoming a source of stress or secrecy in the family, that's also a signal to reconsider how much, or whether, you bet.

What Genuine Desks Usually Ask For

Desks differ, but a reputable desk will normally ask for very little. For a new ID, that's typically your name, a username and the phone number you use every day. For deposits, it's a receipt with the transaction ID. For withdrawals, it's the wallet number and the name on it. Desks with public websites and 24/7 support, such as BetPro Account Pakistan, can explain exactly what they need and why before you send anything. If a desk can't explain why it needs a document, you don't need to send it.

Why Scammers Want Your CNIC

It's worth understanding what criminals do with CNIC images, so you can see why caution matters:

  • SIM registration: attempting to register SIMs in your name, which can then be used for fraud.
  • Wallet accounts: trying to open mobile wallets in your name to receive stolen money.
  • Loan apps: applying for instant loans using your identity.
  • Impersonation: pretending to be you when contacting your contacts or institutions.
  • Social engineering: using your details to make scam calls to your family sound convincing.

Not every request for a CNIC is criminal, but every CNIC image you send increases your exposure. Share only when necessary, only with people you know, and only in a form that limits misuse.

How to Watermark a CNIC Image

  1. Take a clear photo of the card.
  2. Open it in any photo editor on your phone.
  3. Add text across the image, for example: "For BetPro desk withdrawal check only, 30 Sep 2026".
  4. Where allowed, cover information that isn't needed for the stated purpose.
  5. Send it only through your known desk's official contact.
  6. Delete it from your chat after it has been checked, if possible.

A watermark doesn't make an image impossible to misuse, but it makes it much less useful for other purposes.

Myths About CNIC and Withdrawals

"You Must Upload Your CNIC to Withdraw"

Many desks pay withdrawals to a wallet in your name without any CNIC image. If a desk does ask, it should be for a clear reason, and it should never charge for it.

"The Government Checks Every Betting Withdrawal"

Scammers use this idea to demand "tax clearance" fees. No legitimate process involves paying tax through a dealer's personal wallet.

"Using a Relative's Wallet Keeps My Betting Private"

It does the opposite. SMS alerts, wallet reviews and account questions go to your relative, not to you.

"A Selfie With CNIC Is Standard"

Selfie-with-CNIC requests are a common identity theft technique when they come from informal dealers or "support" accounts. Be very cautious.

What to Do If a Withdrawal Is Held

  1. Ask your desk exactly why it's held and what they need.
  2. Send deposit receipts and confirm the wallet name.
  3. If they ask for a CNIC image, check that the request comes from your known desk and ask why.
  4. Never pay a fee to release a withdrawal.
  5. If the desk stops replying or keeps adding conditions, stop depositing and escalate.

Setting Up Correctly From Day One

If you're opening a BetPro ID for the first time, a few minutes of setup avoids almost every identity problem later:

  1. Make sure you have a mobile wallet or bank account in your own name.
  2. Register your ID with the phone number you always carry.
  3. Make your first deposit from your own wallet and save the receipt.
  4. Tell your desk which wallet you'll use for withdrawals, and keep it the same.
  5. Test a small withdrawal to that wallet before depositing more.
  6. Keep all receipts in one folder.

With this setup, you'll rarely, if ever, be asked for extra proof, and any question about ownership can be answered with a few screenshots.

Young People and Family Accounts

Some teenagers get into betting by using a parent's or older sibling's wallet. BetPro is for adults only, and using a family member's account to bet hides the activity from the very people who could help. If you're a parent, keep an eye on unusual transfers from your wallet, especially to personal numbers, and talk openly with young family members about betting and scams.

Signs Betting Is Becoming a Problem

  • Using other people's accounts to deposit or withdraw.
  • Borrowing wallets or money to keep betting.
  • Hiding transactions from family.
  • Spending more than you planned, or chasing losses.
  • Feeling anxious about account checks or SMS alerts.

If these sound familiar, take a break. Your desk can close or suspend your ID, and talking to someone you trust can help.

Bank Transfers vs Mobile Wallets

Both bank accounts and mobile wallets are registered to a person, so the same principle applies: use an account in your own name. There are a few practical differences:

  • Speed: wallet transfers are usually quicker; bank transfers can take longer, especially outside banking hours.
  • Names: bank transfers show the account title clearly, which makes name mismatches obvious.
  • Records: banks provide statements that make it easy to prove past deposits.
  • Visibility: betting-related transfers on a bank account may be more visible to banks, family members with access or employers who pay salaries into that account.

Many users keep a separate personal wallet just for betting money, holding only what they've decided they can afford to lose. That also makes budgeting much easier.

What Happens If a Deposit Is Reported or Reversed

Occasionally a deposit is reported as fraud, for example because it came from a hacked account, or because the sender disputes it. When that happens, the desk may freeze the ID while it investigates, and any winnings linked to that deposit may be withheld. Users who deposit only from their own wallets are almost never affected. Users who accept money from others, or let others use their ID, are the ones who get caught up in these problems.

Checklist Before Every Withdrawal

  • The wallet I'm withdrawing to is in my own name.
  • It's the same wallet I deposited from, or I've told my desk about the change.
  • My available balance covers the amount (no open exposure holding it back).
  • I'm requesting through my desk's known official contact.
  • Nobody has asked me to pay anything to release the money.

A Short Note on Privacy

Some users worry that keeping everything in their own name makes their betting less private. In practice, the opposite is true. Using your own wallet keeps everything in one place you control. Using relatives' accounts spreads your activity across other people's phones, SMS alerts and statements, which is far more likely to cause awkward questions. If privacy matters to you, the best protection is a single personal wallet, a strong password on your phone, and a modest balance that you withdraw regularly.

When to Walk Away From a Desk

  • It insists on CNIC images and selfies for small, routine withdrawals.
  • It charges any "verification", "tax" or "release" fee.
  • It asks you to send money to a new personal number to "clear" your account.
  • It suggests using someone else's wallet to "speed things up".
  • It won't explain why it needs your documents.

If you see these signs, stop depositing, withdraw what you can to your own wallet, and keep records of everything.

Explaining the Rule to Friends

If a friend asks to use your wallet or wants to use theirs for your betting, a simple, polite answer works: "I keep betting money only in my own account, it avoids problems for both of us." Most people understand. Anyone who pushes hard, offers you money for the use of your wallet, or gets angry when you refuse is showing you exactly why the rule exists.

Finally, remember that identity problems are much easier to prevent than to fix. A few minutes spent opening your own wallet and keeping receipts can save days of delays, awkward family conversations and the risk of your documents ending up in the wrong hands.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a CNIC to open a new BetPro ID?

Most desks don't ask for one at registration. Your wallet, which is linked to your CNIC, effectively identifies you.

Can I withdraw to my brother's account?

Some desks may allow it, but it often causes delays and disputes. Use a wallet in your own name.

Why does my desk want the wallet name to match?

To prove ownership, prevent fraud and avoid disputes over who the money belongs to.

Is it safe to send my CNIC photo to my desk?

Only to your known desk, only when there's a clear reason, and preferably with a watermark. Never to anyone who contacted you first.

Someone asked for a CNIC verification fee. Should I pay?

No. Genuine withdrawals don't need fees. It's a scam.

Can someone use my wallet to receive their betting money?

Don't allow it. Your account could be flagged and you could be held responsible.

I don't have my own wallet. What should I do?

If you're an adult, open one in your own name through the provider's official channels. If you can't, don't bet.

What if my CNIC has been misused?

Contact your bank and wallet provider immediately and consider reporting it to the authorities.

Can I deposit from one wallet and withdraw to another?

If both are in your name, it's usually manageable. Tell your desk first and keep receipts from both.

Why was my withdrawal held for checking?

Often because the withdrawal wallet name differed from the deposit name, or the amount was unusually large. Share receipts to resolve it.

Can I use a shop's wallet to deposit?

Cash deposits into your own wallet at a franchise are fine. Avoid sending money to a desk directly from someone else's account.

I'm overseas. Can my family receive my withdrawals?

It's better to use an account in your own name and keep family out of betting transactions.
